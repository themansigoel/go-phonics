How Children Learn to Blend Sounds: Decoding & Reading Fluency

Updated September 24, 2026 • By GoPhonics Literacy Specialists • 6 min read

Blending is the cognitive process of joining individual speech sounds (phonemes) together to form a recognizable word. For instance, putting together /s/, /a/, and /t/ to pronounce "sat".

While sound blending comes naturally to some children, others struggle to hold multiple sounds in working memory long enough to merge them. Here is how children learn blending and how you can help when they get stuck.

The Stages of Sound Blending

  1. Oral Blending (No Print): Hearing sounds spoken by an adult (/s/ - /u/ - /n/) and orally guessing the word ("sun!").
  2. Segmented Blending: Saying each printed letter sound separately (/c/ ... /a/ ... /t/) with pauses in between.
  3. Continuous Blending (Smooth Blending): Singing or stretching sounds smoothly into one another without pausing ("cccaaaattt").
  4. Automatic Decoding: Recognizing sound patterns effortlessly so reading becomes automatic.

The Power of "Continuous Blending"

If your child forgets the first sound by the time they reach the third sound, switch to continuous blending. Teach them to hold continuous sounds (like /s/, /m/, /f/, /a/) without stopping their voice between letters:

Instead of: /m/ (pause) /a/ (pause) /t/
Practice: "mmmm-aaaa-t"

Multisensory Techniques for Tricky Blenders

  • Arm Tapping: Tap the shoulder for the first sound, elbow for the middle vowel, and wrist for the final consonant, then slide down the arm to say the whole word.
  • Squishy Card Physics: Drag visual cards physically on-screen toward each other until they snap into a full word.
  • Sound Buttons: Draw dots under each letter. Tap each dot while saying its sound, then draw a line under all three to blend.
