How to Teach Letter Sounds to Kids: Proven 5-Step Method

Updated September 24, 2026 • By GoPhonics Literacy Specialists • 6 min read

Teaching letter sounds is the single most effective way to help a child transition from non-reader to fluent reader. When children connect visual letter symbols (graphemes) to their corresponding sounds (phonemes), decoding printed words becomes second nature.

Rule #1: Teach "Pure" Sounds (Avoid Schwa)

One of the most common mistakes parents make is adding an extra "uh" sound (called a schwa) to consonants. For example:

  • ❌ Incorrect: Saying "Puh" for P, "Buh" for B, "Muh" for M.
  • ✅ Correct: Clipping the sound crisp and short: /p/ as a soft puff of air, /b/ as a quick stop, /m/ as a humming sound.

If a child learns "Puh-Aaa-Tuh", they will try to blend the word as "Puh-ah-tuh" instead of "pat".

Rule #2: Do Not Teach Alphabetical Order A-Z

Instead of teaching A-B-C-D in sequence, introduce letters in groups that can form words immediately:

Recommended First Group: S, A, T, P, I, N
With just these 6 letters, your child can read 20+ words: sat, pat, tap, pin, tin, pit, sip, nap!

5-Step Daily Teaching Framework

  1. See the Letter: Show the child a card or letter bubble.
  2. Hear the Sound: Pronounce the pure phoneme clearly.
  3. Feel the Movement: Pair the sound with an action (e.g. wiggle fingers like a spider for /s/).
  4. Find the Sound: Ask them to spot the letter in an app or picture book.
  5. Trace the Shape: Trace the letter shape in sand, playdough, or on screen.
